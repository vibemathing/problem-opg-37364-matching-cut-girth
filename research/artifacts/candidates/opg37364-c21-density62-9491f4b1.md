## B1. Exact artificial regularization lemma

Let H be a finite simple bipartite graph with parts X,Y, each of
size N. Let r>=0 be an integer and suppose maximum_degree(H)<=r
and girth(H)>=g>=3. Fix an integer k>=1 and assume kN is even. Put
  D=r+k,
  B=1+D*sum_{j=0}^{g-3}(D-1)^j,
and suppose N>5B.

Then there is an additional edge set F, all of whose edges join
two vertices of X or two vertices of Y, such that:
  F has degree exactly k at EVERY old vertex,
  J=H+F is simple and has girth at least g.
No original edge of H is removed. J need not be bipartite.

Proof. Choose F with maximum cardinality among all artificial edge
sets satisfying degree at most k and simplicity/girth of J. The
empty set is admissible, and the universe of possible pairs is finite.
Thus such a maximum exists. A lexicographically first maximum may
be selected if a deterministic definition is desired.

Every J has maximum degree at most D. For any vertex u, the ball
of radius g-2 has at most B vertices, by the finite count of walks
without immediate reversals. Distances refer to the CURRENT J.
An infinite distance, when present, also satisfies the lower bounds
used below.

Call an old vertex unsaturated if its degree in F is less than k.
In either part P=X or P=Y there are at most B unsaturated vertices.
Otherwise choose an unsaturated u and another unsaturated v outside
its radius-(g-2) ball. The absent edge uv can be added: the degrees
remain within cap, and any new cycle has length at least g.
This contradicts the maximal cardinality of F.

Fix a part P in which a deficit remains. The total deficit there is
  sum_{v in P}(k-degree_F(v)) = kN-2|F[P]|.
It is a positive EVEN integer. Hence it has at least two units.
Choose vertices u,v carrying these units. They may be distinct;
if only one vertex is used, take u=v at a vertex with deficit at
least two. This coincident-centre case is essential.

Since at most B vertices in P are unsaturated,
  2|F[P]| >= k(N-B) > 4kB,
so |F[P]|>2kB.
The union of the two radius-(g-2) balls centred at u and v has
at most 2B vertices. At most 2kB artificial edges of F[P] touch
this union, because artificial degrees are at most k.
Therefore an edge xy of F[P] has both endpoints outside both balls.

Write J0=J-xy. Replace the artificial edge xy by the two edges
  ux and vy,
using ux and uy when u=v. The endpoints x,y are distinct from
u,v, and the new pairs were absent because the relevant distances
in J were at least g-1>=2. The new edge set is simple. Degrees at
x,y are unchanged; the chosen deficit units at u,v are filled.
The number of artificial edges has increased by one.

It remains to prove the girth statement for this switch. In J0:
- every path joining one of {u,v} to one of {x,y} has length at
  least g-1, by the distances in J;
- every x-y path has length at least g-1, since with the former
  edge xy it would otherwise give a cycle shorter than g in J.

A new simple cycle with just one added edge consists of that edge
and an old path between its endpoints, and has length at least g.
For a cycle using both added edges, consider first u!=v. Removing
those two edges from the cycle leaves one of exactly two pairings:
  a u-v path and an x-y path; or
  a u-y path and a v-x path.
In the first pairing the x-y path has length at least g-1 and
the u-v path at least one, so the cycle has length at least g+2.
In the second pairing both paths have length at least g-1, so
its length is at least 2g. If u=v, a cycle using both added
edges is an x-y path with the two edges through u, and has
length at least g+1. A missing path gives no cycle and causes
no exception. Cycles with neither added edge already lie in J0.

Thus the switch preserves girth>=g and all degree caps while
increasing |F|, a contradiction. No positive deficit remains
in either part. This proves B1.

## B2. Why the hypotheses of the exchange are not optional

The parity kN even cannot be deleted: a k-regular graph on N
vertices has degree sum kN=2|E|. In particular odd N cannot
support a 1-regular artificial graph, irrespective of the
available distances or the size of the ambient graph.

An inclusion-maximal edge set alone does not complete this proof.
It prevents adding one edge, but the contradiction needs a
maximum-cardinality set or a process allowing the displayed switch.
The far artificial edge must be chosen using current J, not H alone.

The short-cycle analysis does not assume that deleting xy disconnects
its endpoints; it uses that any remaining x-y path has length at
least g-1. Both possible pairings of a cycle containing the two new
edges are considered. The proof also explicitly handles two deficit
units at the same vertex. Only ARTIFICIAL edges are switched; a
core edge must never be removed under the name of this lemma.

## B4. Final simple bipartite graph and exclusion of matching cuts

Replace each artificial edge uv in F by u-w_uv-v using one new,
distinct vertex per artificial edge. Keep all original edges of H'.
Let G be the resulting graph.

The endpoints of each artificial edge were in the same old part;
put its new vertex in the opposite part. The old edges still go
between the original parts. This proves bipartiteness.

The graph is simple: the core is simple, all artificial pairs are
distinct, and each new vertex is distinct with two different old
neighbours. Every cycle of G suppresses to a cycle of J=H'+F of
no greater length. Distinct old vertices on the cycle stay distinct;
a putative two-edge projected cycle would require parallel edges
in simple J. Thus girth(G)>=g.

It is 2-connected. Deleting an old vertex x leaves H'-x connected
and each new vertex attached to at least one surviving old endpoint.
Deleting one new vertex leaves H' connected and all other new
vertices attached. The graph has at least three vertices. This
verifies the vertex-deletion definition of 2-connectivity directly.

An MC colouring of G cannot use both colours on H', since its
restriction would be an MC colouring of that core. If all old
vertices have the same colour, every new vertex of the opposite
colour has two distinct crossing neighbours, violating the matching
condition. If no new vertex has the opposite colour, the two shores
are not both nonempty. These cases exhaust all possibilities.

There are t>0 new vertices, each of degree two. Old degrees are
between 22+k and 24+k. Consequently
  minimum_degree(G)=2, maximum_degree(G)<=24+k.
The induced old subgraph is H' of minimum degree at least 22.
Hence both maximum average degree and degeneracy are at least 22,
without imposing that lower bound on the GLOBAL average degree.

## B5. Strict density, including the integer endpoint at d0=5

The vertex and edge counts are n+t and m+2t. Therefore
  average_degree(G)=4+(2m-4n)/(n+t).
Using (12), (13), and minimum_degree(H')>=22, we obtain
  4 < average_degree(G)
    <= 4+40/(k+2)-4/((k+2)n)
     < 4+40/(k+2).                                        (14)

For any real d>4 choose
  k=max(1,ceil(40/(d-4))-2).
Then k+2>=40/(d-4), so the strict inequality in (14) gives
average_degree(G)<d, including when the final comparison
4+40/(k+2)<=d is an equality. This is an existence proof for
real d, not an oracle for an unencoded real input.

For the root, fix d0=5 and k=38 before choosing g. Equations (12)-(13)
give t=19n, n_G=20n, and
  2m_G=2m+4t <= 24n-2+76n = 5n_G-2.                       (15)
Thus 2m_G+2<=5n_G is an exact integer certificate of the strict
average bound. Meanwhile maximum_degree(G)<=24+38=62.
Every integer g>=3 has such a finite simple 2-connected bipartite
witness with no matching cut. This is the complete negative-root
quantifier pattern, not a claim based on testing finitely many g.

The extra single-edge deletion in C21A is needed for this use of
k=38: without a strict seed deficit, the same regularization counts
would give only average_degree(G)<=5. The weak inequality does
not meet the frozen hypothesis <5. Omitting that deletion would
leave a genuine proof gap.

## B6. Explicit order bound without a nested girth input

  B=1+62*sum_{j=0}^{g-3}61^j <= 62^(g-1).
For example, compare each term 62*61^(ell-1) with 62^ell
and sum the geometric series through ell=g-2.

Here

For g>=3, this even integer exceeds 1000, K, and 5B+1:
the prefactor is at least 144, the base 384g^2 exceeds 62,
